# Investor map

## The strategic answer first: chase pilots, not capital

**This is the wrong half-year to arrive in Riyadh raising money, and the right one to
arrive selling.**

**H1 2026: Saudi startups raised $219m across 72 deals — funding down 74% year-on-year,
deals down 41%, with no late-stage rounds recorded at all.** Saudi Arabia's share of MENA
funding collapsed from 49% (H1 2025) to **16%**, while its share of deal *volume* held at
34% — meaning the cheques got much smaller, not fewer. **FinTech took 67% of all funding.**
The top ten rounds were ~64% of total capital. Saudi-based investors supplied 74% of it as
international participation retreated.

For contrast, FY2025 was a record: **$1.72bn across 257 deals, up 145%**, first in MENA for
the third consecutive year. You have arrived one year after the peak.

> **A foreign, early-stage, non-fintech AI company is close to the least fundable profile
> in this market right now.** *(The data is sourced; that conclusion is inference from it.)*

**But the buying budget is enormous and reachable.** The $14.9bn announced at LEAP 2025 and
the $42.4bn cumulative are capex — data centres, cloud regions, chips, enterprise
transformation. **Saudi Arabia is not short of budget; it is short of things to spend it
on.** Enterprises and ministries have allocated money and adoption targets to hit before
December. **That money is reachable by a small company. A Series A is not.**

### What Saudi funds want before they write

| Requirement | Reality |
|---|---|
| **KSA traction** | The highest-weighted signal. Pilots with Saudi enterprises, or a local distribution partner. |
| **A Saudi anchor customer** | Effectively required for a foreign founder. One named logo changes the conversation more than any metric. |
| **Local entity** | Not always needed to start a conversation; **required to close.** Many Saudi funds are CMA-licensed with local-domicile mandates. |
| **Vision 2030 narrative** | Explicitly. A generic MENA deck reads as tourism. |
| **Founder presence in Riyadh** | Trust-based, referral-driven ecosystem. **Repeat visits beat one visit.** |
| **A referral in** | Cold outreach badly underperforms a warm intro from an SVC GP, an accelerator or a portfolio founder. |

### So the trip goal is

1. One named Saudi enterprise or government **pilot** — even at LOI stage.
2. One **non-dilutive** path opened — an NTDP conversation with a follow-up booked.
3. One **ecosystem sponsor** — a 500 Global / Sanabil / SVC introduction, which also
   unlocks the MISA Entrepreneur Licence support letter.
4. Every VC meeting treated as relationship-building for a 2027 raise, with **one
   question**: *"What would I need to show you in nine months for this to be a yes?"* Then
   send quarterly updates against that answer. In a referral-driven ecosystem, that is what
   converts.

## ⚠ Where investors actually are

**VCs do not man booths.** The booth-crawl model that works for customers fails completely
for investors. They circulate:

- **LEAP Connect** — the networking zone (Priority / Founder / Investor passes)
- **The Investor Lounge** — **Investor Pass only; you cannot follow them in.** Catch them
  at the boundary.
- **The Rocket Fuel Stage** — sit in the audience during pitch sessions. **The people
  around you are the investors you're trying to meet, and between pitches they are
  stationary and bored.** Highest ratio of investor-minutes to effort at the entire event.
- **Evening dinners and side events.**

**The government bodies — NTDP, Monsha'at, SVC, MISA, SDAIA — *do* man booths with
empowered staff.** They are your highest-yield physical stops by a wide margin.

**A note on the headline number:** LEAP advertises 1,000+ investors from 900+ firms with
**$14.3 trillion combined AUM**. That figure is dominated by J.P. Morgan-, Morgan
Stanley- and General Atlantic-class institutions that will never write a pre-seed cheque.
**The number who could realistically fund you is nearer 100–200.** *(Inference.)*

## Tier A — must hit

**1 · NTDP — `NON-DILUTIVE` · the highest expected value at the entire event**
Saudi's tech-sector funding arm, SAR 2.5bn budget, $430m+ committed to six new initiatives.
Runs **Relocate: up to $1.4m per company** — up to $235k office space, $960k local hiring,
$240k relocation grant. Stated preference for Series A+ or MNCs; precedents include
**Movandi** (US) and **Kneron** ($2m Saudi expansion grant). Also runs four financial
products — Fuel, LendTech, Loan Guarantee, Venture Debt — and NEXT ERA, MVPLab and Saudi
Unicorns. Booth staff are programme managers with real influence.
**Ask:** *"For Relocate, is Series A a hard bar, or is a signed Saudi enterprise contract
an acceptable substitute?"*

**2 · HUMAIN — `DOOR-OPENER`**
Not an investor in the ordinary sense, but the door that makes every other Saudi
conversation easier. See `03-targets-government.md` for the partnership framing and the
warning that comes with it.

**3 · SVC (Saudi Venture Capital Company) — `DOOR-OPENER`**
Government fund-of-funds, SAR 2.8bn, **$3bn total investment capacity, 50+ underlying
funds**, investing up to 65% of a fund's size plus direct co-investment. **SVC is an LP in
most of the Saudi funds you want to meet.** An SVC officer can tell you in ninety seconds
which of their GPs actually invests in your category and stage — worth more than any single
VC meeting.
**Ask:** *"Of your GPs, which three write pre-seed or seed cheques into enterprise AI with
a non-Saudi founding team?"*

**4 · 500 Global MENA — `RAISE` · the most foreign-founder-friendly institution here**
Riyadh-based fund backed by two PIF units and SVC, led by **Amjad Ahmad**, **invests up to
$10m**, regional HQ in Riyadh. Runs the Sanabil accelerator. Global norms, no allergy to a
non-Saudi cap table, and the on-ramp into the PIF orbit.
**Ask:** *"Is 500 MENA the right first cheque for a company like mine, or should I go
through the Sanabil accelerator first?"* — flatters and qualifies simultaneously.

**5 · Wa'ed Ventures (Aramco) — `RAISE` + anchor customer**
Aramco's **$500m** fund with **$100m earmarked for AI** over three years, plus an AI
advisory board drawn from Meta, Amazon, MIT and Oxford. **Uniquely, Wa'ed can pair capital
with Aramco as a customer** — the most valuable anchor logo in the Kingdom.
**Ask:** *"Does Wa'ed pair investment with a business-unit pilot?"*

**6 · Sanabil Investments (PIF) — `RAISE` + `DOOR-OPENER`**
PIF's venture arm, ~**$3bn per annum**, direct investments seed → Series C including AI and
enterprise software. **The realistic route in is the accelerator, not the fund:** the
**Sanabil Accelerator by 500 Global** — 12 weeks, Riyadh, **$100k+**, Batch 11 took 8 from
690+ applications and skewed AI-heavy. Ask when Batch 12 opens.

**7 · STV — `RAISE`, but Series A+**
**Largest VC in the Middle East, $1.4bn+ AUM.** Launched a **$100m AI Fund backed by
Google**, explicitly for **application-layer AI-native startups in MENA** — thesis-perfect
for you, but Series A and up.
**Play it honestly:** *"I'm not pitching you for this round. What would you need to see at
Series A from a company selling AI agents into Gulf enterprises?"* Then send quarterly
updates. If you have real ARR, push hard — this is the single best thesis fit on the list.

**8 · Raed Ventures — `RAISE` · best stage fit of the Saudi funds**
Seed and early-stage, ~**$450m AUM**, backs SaaS, fintech, productivity platforms.
**Ask:** *"Do you require a Saudi CR before you'd term-sheet?"*

**9 · Impact46 — `RAISE`** · Riyadh multi-asset manager, seed → Series A.
**Ask:** *"How many new seed deals have you done in the last six months?"* — after a 74%
market contraction, this question instantly reveals who is actually deploying.

**10 · Merak Capital — `RAISE`** · Riyadh, CMA-licensed, Saudi seed stage, runs accelerator
cohorts.

## Tier B — hit if time

- **Prosperity7 Ventures (Aramco Ventures)** — Fund I $1bn expanded to $3bn; part of
  Aramco Ventures' $7.5bn / 296 investments. **Genuinely global mandate — one of the few
  Saudi vehicles that will fund a non-Saudi company purely on merit.**
- **Nama Ventures** — **pre-seed specialist, $250k–$1m tickets**, 55+ investments, full CMA
  licence since May 2025. Correct stage if you are pre-revenue.
- **Shorooq Partners** — seed → Series A, ~$3.85m average seed. Launched a **$100m global
  AI innovation fund with Presight**. Wants a Gulf/MENA champion — pitch regional
  dominance, not "we're global."
- **COTU Ventures** — $54m fund, **$500k–$2m pre-seed to seed, UAE B2B software**. *Best
  pure stage-and-sector fit on the whole list*, but UAE-centric.
- **Global Ventures** — Series A, $1–10m. Comfortable with global categories from a
  regional base.
- **BECO Capital** — Fund IV $120m, $1–5m at seed. Wants traction and repeatable GTM.
- **KAUST Innovation Fund** — pre-seed to early Series A, **$100k–$700k typical, up to $2m
  early stage**. Deeptech bias; also gives you campus, labs and a talent pipeline.
- **Nuwa Capital** — $100m+ AUM, pre-seed to Series B.
- **MEVP** — $300m+ AUM, Fund IV $150m, Riyadh office, SVC is an LP.
- **Vision Ventures** — Saudi early-stage, fintech and payments lean. Narrower fit unless
  you have a fintech or security angle.
- **ZAINIAC** — Zain KSA's venture arm; already invested in **Actly** (AI agents for CX).
  A customer and an investor in one conversation. See `05-targets-enterprise.md`.

**Unverified — do not put on a list:** **Rasmal Ventures** (fund terms could not be

## Realistic conversion expectations

A well-run solo founder does **25–40 substantive conversations** across four days, of which
maybe **8–12 are investor conversations**, of which **2–4** produce a follow-up call.

**Term sheets originating at a conference are effectively zero.** Nobody funds you from a
hall meeting. The correct goal is **15–20 named contacts with a specific agreed next step
and a reason to message them on Sunday.** The genuinely high-probability outcomes are the
government ones — NTDP, SVC and MISA conversations can convert to a real programme
application within weeks. *(Calibrated inference.)*

# Targets — banking, fintech and insurance

Saudi financial services splits cleanly into two speeds. The **banks** have the biggest
budgets, real published AI intent, and six-month procurement. The **fintech scale-ups**
have smaller budgets and can sign you this quarter. Work both: the fintechs give you Saudi
logos and cashflow while the bank deals mature.

---

## 1 · Riyad Bank — `DIRECT` · Tier A · **the strongest pure-direct target in the Kingdom**

**Why they lead this list.** Riyad Bank launched the **"Center of Intelligence" in July
2024 — the first dedicated AI centre in the Saudi banking sector.** They are deploying AI
against request-processing cycle time, early fraud detection and proactive decision
analytics, and they have **publicly stated that agentic AI is their next horizon on the
road to 2030**. They gave Arab News a Year-of-AI feature interview in 2026.

> **They have told you, in print, that they are shopping. Almost nobody else on any of
> these lists has done that.**

**The pain — in their own words.** Long customer-request processing cycles. Fraud
detection latency. A stated ambition for AI capability across the *whole* organisation
rather than one team.

**Pitch.** *"AI Credit Operations department"* — a team of agents that ingests a corporate
or SME lending application, extracts and validates documents, checks against policy,
drafts the credit memo, and hands a decision-ready package to a human underwriter.

Second use case: an **AI Complaints & Disputes department** — SAMA-regulated, SLA-bound,
and therefore measurable, which is what gets a pilot renewed.

**Buyer.** Head of the Center of Intelligence · Chief Data & Analytics Officer · Chief
Digital Officer.

**Deal shape.** Paid POC, roughly **SAR 400k–800k**, ten weeks, one process, one hard ROI
metric — cycle-time reduction. *(Deal sizing throughout this file is inference from
regional convention, not a quote.)*

## 2 · Al Rajhi Bank — `DIRECT` · Tier B

**What they are.** The world's largest Islamic bank, with enormous retail volume.

**Why they fit.** Two signals. They already run the **"Rajhi" NLP chatbot** in production
— meaning they have hit the ceiling of rules-based bots and know it. And they **adopted
the ServiceNow AI Platform in 2025** — meaning there is an agent-adjacent budget line and
an integration surface you can plug into rather than displace.

**The pain.** Chatbot containment rates plateauing. Branch and call-centre load.
Sharia-compliance review as a manual bottleneck.

**Pitch.** *"Upgrade Rajhi from chatbot to agent"* — an agent that doesn't just answer but
**executes**: card blocks, limit changes, dispute filing, statement generation — in Saudi
Arabic, inside their existing ServiceNow estate.

**Buyer.** Chief Digital Officer · Head of Contact Centre / Customer Experience · Head of
Enterprise Architecture.

**Deal shape.** Direct POC, or **subcontract under their ServiceNow systems integrator**
if procurement resists a new vendor. Have the second option ready — it is often the faster
path into a large Saudi bank.

## 3 · Saudi fintech scale-ups — `DIRECT` · Tier A · **the only targets who can sign this quarter**

**Tamara · Tabby · Foodics · Geidea · HyperPay · Rasan**

**Why they are Tier A despite small deal sizes.** VC-backed. English-speaking founders and
CTOs. **No Etimad requirement, no Regional HQ requirement, no six-month procurement
cycle.** Foodics runs a restaurant OS across 35+ countries; Tamara and Tabby are BNPL at
national scale; Geidea does POS and digital banking tech; Rasan is a listed insurtech
backed by Impact46.

**The pain.** Support ticket volume growing faster than headcount. Coverage across Arabic,
English, Urdu and Tagalog. Merchant onboarding and KYC backlogs. Collections, for the BNPL
businesses.

**Pitch — pick by company.**
- **Geidea, Foodics, HyperPay** → *"AI merchant-onboarding department"*: document
  collection, KYB checks, exception handling.
- **Tamara, Tabby** → *"AI collections and dispute agent"*: Arabic, tone-controlled,
  SAMA-compliant. Tone control matters more than you think in collections here.

**Buyer.** CTO · VP Engineering · Head of CX or Operations. **Often the founder directly**
— and founders walk the floor at LEAP.

**Deal shape.** **$30k–$120k annual**, land-and-expand, two-to-four-week POC, light-touch
or self-serve.

> **Use these to build Saudi logos and cashflow while the enterprise deals mature.** One
> named Saudi customer changes every other conversation in this dossier — including the
> investor ones, where a Saudi anchor customer is close to a precondition for a foreign
> founder. See `08-investor-map.md`.

## 4 · Digital-native banks — stc Bank and D360 — `DIRECT` · bench, but fast

**stc Bank** was named Best Digital Bank in Saudi Arabia in January 2026. **D360 Bank**
is PIF-backed with SAR 1.65bn capital and passed **2 million customers in 2025**.

**Why they are worth a stand visit.** Digital-natives have the **fastest procurement
cycles in Saudi financial services**. They also have no legacy contact-centre estate to
protect, which removes the most common internal objection.

---

## 5 · SNB (Saudi National Bank) — `DIRECT` · bench

Largest bank by assets, investing in enterprise AI frameworks. **Public signal is thin
compared with Riyad Bank.** Worth a card and a follow-up, not a scheduled meeting.

**Also on the bench:** **Alinma** and **SAB** — mid-tier banks that tend to follow Riyad
Bank's lead. If Riyad Bank pilots, these two become warm in the same quarter. Mention
that dynamic to them; competitive framing works in this sector.

---

## 6 · Insurance — Tawuniya and Bupa Arabia — `DIRECT` · Tier C

Saudi Arabia's largest insurers. Tawuniya has invested in cloud and AI-driven platforms.
Bupa Arabia is expanding into owned integrated clinics, which adds operational load.

**The pain.** Claims adjudication volume. Provider network queries. Pre-authorisation
turnaround.

**Pitch.** *"AI claims-adjudication department"* — agents that read the claim, the policy
and the clinical notes, apply the rules, **auto-approve the clean 70%**, and escalate the
rest with a drafted rationale. Requires Arabic plus medical coding, which is a real moat
if you can do it.

**Buyer.** Chief Operating Officer · Chief Digital Officer · Head of Claims.

**Why only Tier C.** Insurance procurement in the Kingdom is slow and heavily regulated,
and neither company has published an agentic-AI intent signal the way Riyad Bank has.
Textbook use case, unproven urgency.

## Sector-wide objection you will hit here

Every regulated financial institution will raise **data residency and PDPL** before they
raise price. Have the in-Kingdom deployment architecture on one slide, and know that
SDAIA has issued **48 enforcement decisions across 2025–2026** with fines up to **SAR 5m
per breach**. See `07-objections-and-context.md`.

The second objection is **"we already have ServiceNow / Salesforce Agentforce."** Both are
genuinely present in this market — Agentforce passed 6,000+ paid deals globally by March
2026, and ServiceNow AI is already inside Al Rajhi. Do not fight the platform. Position as
the **department-level composition layer that runs inside their existing estate.**
